QarJol: a winter campus courier robot (A* + Bayes + MDP)
All numbers are classroom assumptions; the map is a hypothetical campus.

========================================================================
PART 1 - A* SEARCH: which route can the robot take?
========================================================================
INPUT   6x6 campus grid, 4-connected moves, cost 1 per step,
        start S=(5, 4), goal G=(0, 3), snow-prone passage P=(2, 5)
        heuristic h(n) = Manhattan distance to G (admissible)

. . . G . .
. C . . . .
. M M M M P
. . . . . .
. L L . . .
. . . . S .

STEPS   A* expansions for the shortcut (node, g, h, f = g + h):
        (5, 4)  g=0  h=6  f=6 
        (4, 4)  g=1  h=5  f=6 
        (3, 4)  g=2  h=4  f=6 
        (3, 3)  g=3  h=3  f=6 
        (4, 3)  g=2  h=4  f=6 
        (5, 3)  g=1  h=5  f=6 
        (3, 2)  g=4  h=4  f=8 
        (3, 5)  g=3  h=5  f=8 
        (2, 5)  g=4  h=4  f=8 
        (1, 5)  g=5  h=3  f=8 
        (0, 5)  g=6  h=2  f=8 
        (0, 4)  g=7  h=1  f=8 
        (0, 3)  g=8  h=0  f=8 

        A* expanded 13 nodes; uniform-cost search (h=0) expanded 24 for the same length 8.

OUTPUT  shortcut through P (8 steps): (5,4) -> (4,4) -> (3,4) -> (3,5) -> (2,5) -> (1,5) -> (0,5) -> (0,4) -> (0,3)
. . . G * *
. C . . . *
. M M M M P
. . . . * *
. L L . * .
. . . . S .

        if P is blocked, the robot sees it at (3, 5) after 3 steps and A* re-plans without P: total 14 steps
* * * G . .
* C . . . .
* M M M M X
* * * * * *
. L L . * .
. . . . S .
EXPLAIN A* finds the shortest routes, and their lengths become the rewards of
        the MDP; the Manhattan heuristic lets it skip useless cells.

========================================================================
PART 2 - BAYES: is the passage blocked by snow?
========================================================================
INPUT   prior P(Blocked) = 0.30 (overnight snow forecast)
        sensor: P(SNOW|Blocked) = 0.85, P(SNOW|Clear) = 0.15
        observed reading = SNOW
STEPS   P(reading|Blocked) * P(Blocked) = 0.85 * 0.30 = 0.2550
        P(reading|Clear)   * P(Clear)   = 0.15 * 0.70 = 0.1050
        P(reading) = 0.2550 + 0.1050 = 0.3600
OUTPUT  P(Blocked | SNOW) = 0.2550 / 0.3600 = 0.7083
EXPLAIN Bayes combines the forecast with a noisy sensor, so the robot neither
        trusts one reading blindly nor ignores it.

========================================================================
PART 3 - MDP: Move now or Wait for the snowplow?
========================================================================
INPUT   states Clear / Blocked (+ terminal Delivered); actions Move, Wait
        delivery +20, step -1, stuck penalty -3, wait -1, gamma 0.95
        Wait transitions: Clear->Blocked 0.10 (new drift), Blocked->Clear 0.10 (snowplow)
        rewards R(s,a) from the A* route lengths:
          R(Clear, Move)   = 20 - 8 = +12
          R(Blocked, Move) = 20 - 14 - 3 = +3
          R(any, Wait)     = -1
STEPS   value iteration: V(s) = max_a [R(s,a) + gamma * sum T(s,a,s') V(s')]
          sweep 0: V(Clear)=0.0000  V(Blocked)=0.0000
          sweep 1: V(Clear)=12.0000  V(Blocked)=3.0000
          sweep 2: V(Clear)=12.0000  V(Blocked)=3.0000
          ... converged after 2 sweeps
          Q(Clear, .) = Move 12.0000, Wait 9.5450  -> best Move
          Q(Blocked, .) = Move 3.0000, Wait 2.7050  -> best Move
OUTPUT  if the state were known: Clear -> Move, Blocked -> Move
        with belief b = P(Blocked): Move is best for every b
EXPLAIN The MDP weighs a quick delivery against getting stuck, and counts what
        can happen while waiting (snowplow or new snow).
